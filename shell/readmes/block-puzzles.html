Block Puzzles

Sliding block puzzles, starting with a take on the mobile game Block Out: slide colored blocks around a crowded board and out through the doors that match their color. There's no clock: take as long as you like.

Live at https://rivukis.github.io/Rivukis-Website/block-puzzles/.

How to play

  • Drag a block with your finger or mouse. It follows you smoothly, in any direction, diagonally too, as long as there's room. It slides along anything in its way and never passes through other blocks or walls. If you pull it toward a gap it's sliding past, it drops into the gap, however fast you drag. It moves fast, but glides rather than jumps. Let go and it settles into the nearest squares.
  • Every board starts full, with one block already at its door. Tap it to send it out and make room.
  • Boards come in all shapes: rectangles, odd shapes with corners and notches cut out, and boards with walls (raised purple squares) inside: single pillars, bigger 2x2 or 2x3 pillars, and longer walls that partly split the board into rooms.
  • Doors are colored notches in the board's outline (two never meet at an inside corner) (testing mode can show other looks). A door is never wider than the widest block of its color.
  • A block leaves as soon as it's in its door's spot: right up against a door of its own color (or within a quarter of a square of it), all of it in front of the door, with nothing in the way, even while you're still dragging. Let go of a block that would settle at its door, and it goes straight out from where it is. It crumbles away as it goes through the wall, front first, with a crumbling sound (the speaker button at the top turns sound off). Once the last block of a color has gone, that color's doors fade away.
  • Blocks only leave when you touch them. If moving one block clears the way for another that's already at its door, tap that one to send it out.
  • Clear the board of blocks to win.
  • Blocks look like toy bricks, with studs on top, and glow while you hold one. There are seven colors, chosen to be easy to tell apart: red, yellow, green, sky blue, blue, purple and pink.
  • Each color usually has just one door. A color only gets a second door when one of its blocks couldn't use the first: it's too wide for it, or walls keep it from getting there. Two doors of a color are never on the same side of a wall.

Packs and levels

Levels come in packs, laid out like the level packs in Flow Free:

  • Classic - Beginner, Easy, Medium, Hard and Very hard: 30 levels each, one pack for each difficulty. How hard a level is is measured by how much juggling it takes: moving blocks out of the way to make room, rather than straight out. Beginner levels need little or none; Very hard ones need 45 moves or more of it. Boards grow with difficulty, from 4 by 4 up to 6 wide by 8 tall. In each pack, levels 1 to 10, 11 to 20 and 21 to 30 were each made a different way (see below).
  • Alpha: every level made before the Classic packs (230 of them), in one pack, easiest first.
  • Shapes: 30 levels on boards of all shapes (corners cut off, L's, T's, crosses, U's, staircases, notches), Easy to Medium. The first of a pack for each new feature.
  • Frozen Doors: 30 levels where three or more doors are iced over, showing how many more blocks have to leave before they thaw. Easy to Medium; from level 11, on boards of all shapes too.
  • Lids: 30 levels where lids hide whole sections of the board (the blocks under them can't be seen or moved) until enough other blocks have left, almost levels in one. A lid always has two sides against the board's edge, a wall, or a lid that comes off no sooner, so the order they come off matters. Lids come off in phases: the first after at least 3 blocks have left, each one after at least 3 more; in some levels only once you've cleared everything you can reach. What a lid shows is never easier than the level, and every lid has walls beside it, so what's under it is a real next part of the level. Easy to Medium; from level 11, with shapes and frozen doors too.
  • One Way: 30 levels with blocks marked with an arrow that only slide across, or only up and down, and can only leave that way. Easy to Medium; from level 11, with shapes, frozen doors and lids too.
  • Stars: 30 levels where starred blocks can only leave through a starred door of their color (any block can use a starred door). Easy to Medium; from level 11, with the features before too.
  • Factory Floor: 30 levels with patches of floor (at least 2 by 3, and two or three on bigger boards) marked in orange stripes: once nothing is on one, its doors slide open and a new set of blocks rises up, to clear too. While you hold a block off one, it lights up: letting go (or the block leaving) would open it. Easy to Medium; from level 11, with the features before too.
  • Wall Factory: 30 levels with a door in the wall, outlined in the color of the block it holds next, that pushes in new blocks one at a time, as soon as there's room for the next: its doors fold open to let each one out. Hold a block to see where the next will come in; the spot lights up when letting go would let it in. Blocks slide out a row at a time, so an L comes out foot last. Easy to Medium; from level 11, with the features before too.
  • Keys and Gates: 30 levels with gates, short stretches of wall in a color with a keyhole, that open once the key blocks of their color have left. Every gate is in the way: you can't finish without opening it, and it holds you up before its key is out, so you have to go and get the key out for it. Some shut off part of the board, most shut a door. Easy to Medium; from level 11, with the features before too.
  • Gem Locks: 30 levels with blocks in chains, held by a padlock with a gem in it (ruby, emerald, sapphire, pearl, amethyst or topaz). Other blocks carry that gem: as each leaves, its gem flies to the padlock, and once all have (the padlock counts them down), the chains fall away. Easy to Medium; from level 11, with the features before too.
  • Linked Blocks: 30 levels with pairs of blocks of different colors linked together, side by side, that move as one until one of them leaves. Easy to Medium; from level 11, with the features before too.
  • Two-Color Blocks: 30 levels with blocks in two colors, in a spiral (top and bottom one color, left and right the other), that can leave through a door of either. Easy to Medium; from level 11, with the features before too.
  • Layered Blocks: 30 levels with blocks in two layers: through a door of the outer color they lose that layer, and then leave through a door of the inner one. Easy to Medium; from level 11, with the features before too.
  • Test: Hybrid 2: levels for testing, Easy to Very hard, made by the newest generator, which aims at how hard a level should feel rather than at how much juggling it takes (see below).
  • Experiment: Local juggles: 15 levels for testing (5 Easy, 5 Medium, 5 Hard), made to have more of their juggling among a few blocks in one part of the board, rather than across the whole of it.

In the Classic packs, no piece is bigger than 5 squares (and no board has more than two that big), the colors are mixed (no more than two blocks of a color touch at the start, since a clump of one color clears a lot of room at once), and the block you tap first is always small: mostly a single square or a domino, sometimes a little L. Pieces are never more than 3 squares long in any direction.

The levels are made by computer, three ways:

  • Built up (levels 1-10 of each Classic pack): the game played backward. Starting from an empty board, blocks come in through their doors and get pushed deeper, until the board is full.
  • Hill climbed (levels 11-20): a random full board, changed a little at a time (a block recolored, a few blocks reshaped, a door moved), keeping the changes that bring it closer to the difficulty wanted.
  • Both (levels 21-30): built up, then hill climbed.

New levels are made the last way, both.

Every level is checked by a solver, and goes in the pack its difficulty belongs in.

Since the owner started rating levels, difficulty is measured two ways together: how many choices a level asks for (at each move along the way, how many moves there are against how few of them help), and how many long dead ends it has: wrong turns you can follow ten moves deep, each move opening up the next, without ever freeing a block, until you have to back out. Those turned out to be what makes the hardest levels feel hard. Newer levels are made to have none at Easy, a few at Hard, and many at Very hard.

What each difficulty is meant to feel like:

  • Beginner: no real logic; move the pieces to their doors. It's how the game, or a new feature, is introduced.
  • Easy: a first taste: a few blocks moved out of the way, or several moved together, but the way through is still clear at a glance.
  • Medium: you can see part of the solution, and find the rest along the way.
  • Hard: you have to think: some trial and error, some moving pieces to see what affects what.
  • Very hard: you can get lost, retracing your steps to find the path you missed.
  • Extreme (planned): a step beyond, for those who want to suffer.

Pick a pack, and its levels are shown in pages of 25 (5 across, 5 down). Swipe, or use the arrows, to move between pages; each page has its own color. A solved level shows a star in its square, and a locked one a padlock.

Three levels you haven't solved yet are always open in each pack: at first levels 1 to 3, and each one you solve opens the next. So you can skip a hard one for now and come back to it. Every level has been checked to make sure it can be solved, and your progress is saved in your browser.

Testing mode. While the game is being tested, the button at the top switches between testing mode and normal mode, and remembers your choice. In testing mode every level is open, each level shows how hard it is (its depth: the most moves in a row you have to plan before any block can leave; its juggles: moves that make room rather than take a block out; its effort: how many positions the solver weighed; and, for the testing packs, its choices: how hard the right moves are to find among all the moves you could make), and a "Test values" panel at the top lets you try out how fast a dragged block can move, how fast a block slides out, and different looks for the doors.

Stuck? In testing mode, the 💡 Solve button plays the solution from where you are (half a second a square; the test panel has a slider), with the board locked. Tap "Stop solving" to take over again. A level finished this way doesn't count as solved (no star, no coins), but you can still rate it.

Rating levels. In testing mode, after clearing a level you can say how hard it felt, from 1 (beginner) to 5 (very hard). Ratings are saved in your browser, and the home page has them all as text to copy and send back, with each level's numbers alongside, to tune the level generators.

Coins and boosters

You earn 20 coins the first time you clear a level, and 5 for clearing it again. Spend them on boosters, from the home page:

  • 🔨 Hammer: tap a block to break it and take it off the board.

You start with one.

Features

Each new kind of piece or board part has its own pack (see above), Easy to Medium: the first 10 levels with just that feature, the rest mixing in the ones before it. In order: boards of all shapes, frozen doors, lids, one-way blocks, starred blocks, floor factories, wall factories, keys and gates, gem locks, linked blocks, two-color blocks and layered blocks. Next up: paint pads (floor that recolors a block), one-shot doors (that close after some blocks have gone through), ice floor (that blocks slide across) and ordered blocks (numbered, to leave in order).

A better way to tell colors apart, for colorblind players, is planned too. The home page has the full to-do list, including new colors.

Not real yet

Buying coins isn't real: nothing is charged and no ad is shown. They're written to the Action log at the bottom of the page, so you can see what would happen.
