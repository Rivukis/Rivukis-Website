DM Prep

A prep and play companion for Dungeon Masters. Write up your adventures (room by room), NPCs, monsters, quests, buildings, towns, factions, items, encounters and the players' characters; star what you need for the next session; run the session from one page at the table; and print any of it as cut-out cards.

The tabs at the top are Active session (home), Sessions (all of them), Calendar, one per kind of card, Print, Cheatsheet, and Your data (export and import). The search box searches everything (press Ctrl+K or / to jump to it).

Sessions

One session at a time can be active: the one you're playing or prepping. The Active session tab opens straight to it. With no active session, it lists your sessions (newest first) to pick from.

The Sessions tab lists every session, newest first, each with Make active (or Make inactive for the active one). Click a session to open its page. Every session's page works the same as the active one's, and any session can be changed at any time, past ones included.

Each session has a Played on date, the real-world day you ran it, shown on its page and in the session lists, as well as where it falls in the game world's calendar (below).

On a session's page:

  • Starred cards. Every card has two stars: Every session (always on hand) and, while a session is active, Session N (active), so a card you just made is one tap from the session you're running. A session's page lists what's starred for it, and you can star cards for it there too (type a name) or remove them. Each session keeps its own stars, so an old session still shows what you had ready for it. To print them, use the Print tab.
  • Adventures, room by room. A starred adventure gets its own tab next to ★ Starred. Pick the room the party is in, and you see just that room's enemies (with their stats and pictures), NPCs, items and notes. Pick more rooms when the neighbors hear the fight and come running.
  • Encounters on the fly. The ⚔ Encounters tab lists every encounter: backups, random encounters on the road, anything not tied to a room. Filter by name, place or tag (say "road"), show only the ones not in an adventure yet, pick one or more to see their enemies' stats, tactics and treasure, or press 🎲 Random encounter to roll one from the list.
  • Info and quests. Every NPC's info (rumors, things they know) and quests are listed too, starred NPCs first. Roll a rumor picks one the players haven't heard yet.
  • What the players have been told. Each info and quest item has a box: empty (not told), a green ✓ (told), or ◐ (partly told, with a note on what was said). Tap it to change it. A small number on the box says how many sessions ago it happened; no number means this session.
  • Log. Jot down what happens (names link to their cards); every line can be changed or removed later.
  • Session details. Number, title, notes, and when it happens in the world: a start and end date in your calendar. A new session suggests starting where the one before it ended (one click), but any date works.

When a session stops being active (you make it inactive, or make another one active), you're shown everything starred for it, all ticked: untick what you don't need, pick the session to star them for (the next one, any other, or a new one), and confirm. Or skip it.

Calendar

The Calendar tab shows a month at a time: holidays and events, and sessions on the days they cover in the world. Below it, a session timeline lists sessions in story order with how long each lasted and how many days passed in between.

It uses the American calendar until you make your own on Calendars and events. A custom calendar has your own months (names and lengths), days of the week (as many as you like, with names, and which one year 1 starts on), and leap days, either an extra day at the end of a month or a day of its own after a month, every so many years. Add holidays (every year) and events (one year) to any calendar.

Cards

Every card has its own page to fill in, and a View page made for the table: big text, links, and the told boxes.

Links. Wherever a card's name appears in another card (an NPC's Location, a building's Workers, a rumor), it links to that card, and each card lists where it's Mentioned. Fields that name other cards suggest names as you type; a name that isn't a card yet gets a + button that makes it one, already linked back.

Dice. 🎲 buttons roll a name (by species and gender), species, quirk, motivation and pitfall, a voice, or a building or town name. Quick NPC rolls a half-made NPC (pick an archetype like Guard or Innkeeper for their job and stat block too) for when the players talk to someone you didn't plan for.

Tags and filtering. Give cards tags (comma-separated) and filter a list by name, text or tag, or show just the starred ones.

Pictures. Monsters and player characters can have a picture; it shows on their pages and on the Session tab (not on the printout).

NPC cards

  • Name, Species, Age, Gender, Size, then Profession, Quirk, Stat block, then Goal, Motivation, Pitfall, then Location, Faction.
  • Motivation and Pitfall are picked from twelve traits, with help on how each wins the NPC over or insults them.
  • Info and Quests: lists, with a told box on each item (the printed card has a tick box per item). Each quest line links to a quest card, with an optional line of your own.
  • Background and Notes.
  • Voice: nine boxes (Spatial, Timing, Weight, Tempo, Volume, Tone, Location, Breath, # Words) and a wide Defect box. Spatial, Timing and Weight name a Laban voice style, with characters who sound like it. A box with nothing picked prints blank, to fill in by hand.

Adventures

An adventure has a name, where it is, a summary, notes, and rooms. Each room has a name, its enemies, NPCs, items, encounters and notes. Each enemy has its own boxes for how many, which monster (or NPC) and an optional note ("Ripper", "leader"). Put a prepped encounter in a room and its enemies join the room's. Names are suggested as you type and link to their cards.

Quests

Quests are cards of their own: name, who gives it, status, where, reward, description and notes. On an NPC, each line of Quests names a quest (linked, suggested as you type, or made on the spot) and, if you like, a line in your own words for how this NPC puts it. The told box is on each NPC's line, so you know who has passed it on.

Monsters

Name, type, CR, AC, HP, speed, stat block, attacks and abilities, tactics and notes, and a picture.

Other cards

  • Buildings: name, town, description, workers, backstory, notes.
  • Towns: name, region, size, leader, description, notes.
  • Factions: name, leader, goal, members, allies, enemies, description, notes.
  • Items: name, type, rarity, value, who has it, description, notes.
  • Encounters: name, location, difficulty, enemies (how many, which monster, a note), tactics, treasure, notes. Use tags like "road" or "travel" to group them for the Session tab.
  • Players: character, player, species, class and level, hooks, notes, and a picture.

Printing

The card pages are made for using the site live at the table; everything printable is on the Print tab:

  • A session's starred cards: pick a session (the active one to start with) and download every card starred for it.
  • The cheatsheet.
  • Each kind of card: tick the cards you want (All / None) and download them, or download blank cards of that kind to fill in by hand. A card whose text is too long to fit says so.

Every download shows its pages first, where you pick the paper (Letter or A4) and cards per page. As many as fit (the default) makes each card as tall as what's on it; or pick 3 or 4 to a page. Print at 100% and cut along the dashed lines.

The Cheatsheet tab has the motivations and pitfalls, the Laban voice styles and every voice option, to read on screen.

Keeping your cards

Everything is saved in this browser only. There's no account or server yet, so clearing your browser's data, or using another browser or device, starts from scratch. After your first visit, the site works offline too.

On the Your data tab, Export all downloads one file with every card and session. Keep it somewhere safe. Import file… reads an export back in: cards from the file are added, and any card that's already here is replaced by the file's copy. Nothing is deleted by an import.

On a first visit, a few sample cards from Phandalin are there to show how things work. Delete them whenever you like.
