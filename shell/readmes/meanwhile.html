Meanwhile

A choose-your-own-adventure where choices take real time. Each choice in a story has a wait: choose to wait an hour in the story, and the next part unlocks after an hour of real time.

Live at https://rivukis.github.io/Rivukis-Website/meanwhile/.

How it works

A story is a set of parts joined by choices, and every choice has its own wait, chosen by the author. "Wait by the radio to see if it repeats" might unlock in an hour; "Walk to the other side of the complex" might take 15 minutes, and the next part tells you what happened on the way.

Each part can have text, an image or short animation to look at, and sound to listen to.

For readers

  • Discover lists every published story. Search by title, author or description, then add a free story to your library, or buy a paid one.
  • Your library shows the stories you've added and where you are in each: not started, waiting (with a countdown), ready, or finished.
  • Reading: read a part, pick a choice, and wait. Your place is saved, even mid-wait, so you can close the page and come back when the next part is ready.
  • Your map shows only the parts of the story you've already read, and where you are, so you can choose a different path next time. Show read markers marks the choices you've already taken. Both start hidden each time you open a story.
  • Sound plays on its own when a part has it. The Sound button at the top turns it off and on, and is remembered. Each sound has a written caption too.
  • Start over whenever you like. What you've read is remembered, so the map and read markers help you find the paths you haven't taken.
  • If an author unpublishes a story you already have, you keep it.

For authors

Sign in and open Write to see your stories. Start a new one, open a story file, or open the example story to see how a finished one fits together. The editor walks through four steps:

  1. Story: the title, author, description and cover.
  2. Media: upload sounds, images and animations. Each file gets an id, and can be used on as many parts as you like.
  3. Parts, one at a time: the text, an optional heading, a visual and a sound picked from your uploads, and the choices, each with its wait and the part it leads to. Adding a choice makes a new blank part, and the main button takes you to the next part that still needs writing.
  4. Finish: fix any problems the editor has found, preview the story as a reader would, set a price (or keep it free), and publish.

The Map shows the whole story as a graph, with each choice's wait, and highlights any part with a problem or that no choice leads to. Every part and media file can have a name that only you see, to keep a big story organised; readers only ever see the heading and text.

Only you can edit or export your stories. Readers get a story to read, never your story file. Your work is saved as you type.

Trying it out

This is an early preview, so some things are simulated:

  • Waits are shortened to 3 seconds for testing. The story still shows each choice's real wait ("Wait: 1 hour").
  • Accounts, published stories and libraries exist only in your browser. Signing in takes just a name, with no password, and other people can't see what you publish yet.
  • Payments are pretend: buying a story charges nothing.
  • Uploaded media only shows until you reload the page; the example story's media always works.
  • The Action log at the bottom of the page lists what the site would do for real (save to the server, take a payment, send a notification) once those parts are built.

A good first story is "The Signal at Kestrel Station" (9 parts, 4 endings): add it from Discover to read it, or open it under Write to see how it's built.

Story files

Stories can be exported as and opened from a story.json file. A story has parts (called nodes in the file), the choices between them, each with a wait in seconds, and a list of media files that parts refer to. There is exactly one start, at least one ending, and every part must be reachable from the start. The editor checks all of this as you write.
